# Archetype Templates

{: .note}
> ##### Design Guidelines
> * An archetype **should** be simple.
> * An archetype **should** provide its benefits early.
> * An archetype **should not** introduce new resources.
> * An archetype **should not** have a capstone feature.
> * An archetype **should not** overlap with multiclassing.

The templates in this section can be used when designing new archetypes. Note that they are not mandatory. 


## Fighter

| Level | Name           | Benefit                     |
| ----- | -------------- | --------------------------- |
| 3     | Aura           | Strength Intimidation Bonus |
| 3     | _ Action       | Bonus Action Ability        |
| 7     | _ Counter      | Reaction Ability            |
| 10    | *              | Wildcard                    |
| 15    | Glancing Blows | Half Damage on Miss         |
| 18    | Dauntless      | Action Surge Recharge       |


#### Overview
Fighters suffer from a lack of tactical options in and out of combat. Their archetypes should be provide new ways to interact with the world.

#### Social Challenges
*Aura* helps to open up social encounters by adding their Strength modifier to Intimidation. This benefit allows engagement in those encounters without requiring a high Charisma score, while also rewarding characters that have both.

#### Bonus Actions
The *_ Action* ability adds tactical variety by offering bonus actions. These have the psychological benefit of giving the player more tactics for use during their turn. They also have the gameplay benefit of not interfering with the fighter's typical role (make weapon attacks).

#### Counter
The *_ Counter* ability adds a reaction, giving players a reason to be involved outside of their turn.

## Rogue 

| Level | Name              | Benefit                  |
| ----- | ----------------- | ------------------------ |
| 3     | _ Hands           | Bonus Action Ability     |
| 3     | Trade Secrets: _  | Wildcard                 |
| 9     | *                 | Wildcard                 |
| 13    | Use Mystic Device | +1 Attunement Slot       |
| 17    | Thief’s Reflexes  | Two Turns on First Round |


#### Overview
Rogues have a large gap between their first subclass benefit (level 3) and their second (level 9). Their archetypes should help in bridging this gap.

#### Hands
The *_ Hands* features provide additional uses for a rogue's bonus action. One of these is guaranteed to be **Use an Object**, which allows rogues to throw grenades, apply poisons, and otherwise interact with items. The other actions should reflect the archetype's concept.

#### Trade Secrets
The *Trade Secrets* feature is designed to offset the dearth of features between levels 3 and 9. It provides benefits at three points (levels 3, 5, and 7) and allows players more decision points in their build.

## Wizard

| Level | Name          | Benefit             |
| ----- | ------------- | ------------------- |
| 2     | Scholarship   | Expertise Knowledge |
| 2     | Spell Mastery | At-Will Spell       |
| 6     | *             | Wildcard            |
| 10    | *             | Wildcard            |
| 14    | Deep Reserves | Arcane Recovery x2  |

#### Overview
Wizards have too many spells and abilities to manage at any given time. Their archetypes should focus on enhancing existing abilities over providing new ones.

#### Scholarship
*Scholarship* improves a wizard's knowledge skills. Players gain some roleplay benefits by framing their character as a specialized scholar of Nature, History, or one of the knowledge skills, similar to how fighters choose a fighting style.

#### Spell Mastery
*Spell Mastery* extends an existing ability (a first-level spell) by removing its spell slot cost. It operates identically to certain Eldritch Invocations in the Player's Handbook (*Mask of Many Faces*, *Armor of Shadow*, etc.) The spell in question should be chosen with care. Removing the cost for a combat spell like *Burning Hands* would be a poor choice, while *Disguise Self* or *Detect Magic* are situational enough to make for interesting roleplay tools.

## Cleric (Martial)

| Level | Name              | Benefit                        |
| ----- | ----------------- | ------------------------------ |
| 1     | Spell List        | Spell List                     |
| 1     | Bonus Proficiency | Martial Weapons or Heavy Armor |
| 1     | *                 | Wildcard                       |
| 2     | Channel Divinity  | Channel Divinity               |
| 6     | Qi Weapon         | Increase Weapon Damage         |
| 8     | Divine Strike     | Weapon Damage Increase         |
| 14    | Inner Strength    | Channel Divinity +1            |

#### Overview
Martial clerics are spellswords, supplementing their weapon skills with a modicum of magic. 

#### Spell List
A martial cleric's spells can be thought of as "techniques", as one might see in *Street Fighter* or similar games. They're best suited for providing bursts of firepower or enhancements that build upon the caster's martial talents.

#### Bonus Proficiencies
The *Bonus Proficiency* offers a decision point for what kind of warrior this cleric is. 

#### Channel Divinity
*Channel Divinity* can take many forms, but ideally, it should be an ability with precedence in existing sourcebooks. 

#### Qi Weapon
Martial clerics gradually fall behind fighters and rogues in weapon use. The former gain *Extra Attack* at level 5, while the latter sees incremental improvements in *Sneak Attack*. *Qi Weapon's* modest damage bonus helps to offset that gap without eclipsing other classes.

#### Divine Strike
*Divine Strike* is a comparatively late feature. It brings a martial cleric almost to parity with a fighter of the same level. Unlike a fighter, it is an "all or nothing" roll, lacking the ability to target multiple enemies or benefit from partial success (such as landing the first attack and missing the second).

### Cleric (Spellcaster) 

| Level | Name                | Benefit                     |
| ----- | ------------------- | --------------------------- |
| 1     | Spell List          | Spell List                  |
| 1     | Bonus Proficiency   | Cantrip and Skill Expertise |
| 1     | *                   | Wildcard                    |
| 2     | Channel Divinity    | Channel Divinity            |
| 6     | *                   | Wildcard                    |
| 8     | Potent Spellcasting | Cantrip Damage Increase     |
| 14    | Inner Strength      | Channel Divinity +1         |

#### Overview
Spellcaster clerics are armored casters. They have a narrower selection of magic than a wizard, but compensate with higher health and better equipment.

#### Bonus Proficiency
Clerics with this disposition favor spells over weapons. Given that *Sacred Flame* is the only offensive cantrip in the Cleric list, it's a good idea to borrow one from the wizard spell list.

#### Channel Divinity
*Channel Divinity* can take many forms, but ideally, it should be an ability with precedence in existing sourcebooks. 

#### Potent Spellcasting
*Potent Spellcasting* is a small but noticable boost to cantrip damage. It is a late addition however, and should not be the linchpin of any archetype.
